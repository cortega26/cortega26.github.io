# soporte.tooltician.com — Product, Go-To-Market & Implementation Plan

**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 2026-09-27  
**Status:** Ready for implementation in ChatGPT Work  
**Owner:** Carlos / Tooltician  
**Primary objective:** Launch a trustworthy, measurable, hyperlocal technology-support service for Macul, Ñuñoa and selected sectors of Providencia, capable of generating cash flow quickly without diluting Tooltician's existing B2B positioning.

---

# 1. Executive summary

`soporte.tooltician.com` should not be treated as “a page advertising PC repair.” It should be built as a small, productized local-services business with five tightly integrated layers:

1. **A clear service offer** — narrowly scoped services that Carlos can deliver reliably.
2. **A trust system** — real identity, transparent prices, written authorization, documented work, privacy safeguards and warranty terms.
3. **A conversion-focused local website** — fast, mobile-first, Spanish-first, with WhatsApp as the main conversion path.
4. **An acquisition system** — Google Business Profile, neighborhood Facebook groups, Yapo, selective lead marketplaces, referrals and later paid search.
5. **A measurement + operations loop** — track source → lead → booked visit → completed job → revenue → review → referral, then continuously reallocate effort toward the highest-value acquisition and service categories.

The first version must remain deliberately small. The goal is **not** to build a generic repair shop or a full SaaS platform. The goal is to get the first 10–20 paid jobs, learn the economics, collect genuine reviews and identify the highest-margin repeatable services.

# 2. Strategic thesis

## 2.1 Why this can work

The market has a structural trust problem. Customers often cannot independently evaluate:

- whether the diagnosis is correct;
- whether a proposed repair is necessary;
- whether replaced parts were actually faulty;
- whether their private data is being handled safely;
- whether the final price will match the initial expectation;
- whether the technician will disappear if the problem returns.

That means the product is not merely “technical repair.”

The product is:

> **Competent technology support from a real, identifiable professional, with a diagnosis first, explicit authorization before changes, transparent pricing and documented work.**

Tooltician already provides useful credibility because the parent brand publicly demonstrates real technical work and production systems. The local-support vertical should inherit that credibility without changing the B2B positioning of `tooltician.com`.

## 2.2 Positioning

Recommended public positioning:

> **Tooltician Soporte**  
> Soporte técnico a domicilio, claro y profesional.

Primary service-area message:

> **Macul · Ñuñoa · Providencia**

Primary trust promise:

> **Primero diagnosticamos. Te explicamos qué encontramos y cuánto costará resolverlo antes de realizar trabajos adicionales.**

Supporting promises:

- Atención personal.
- Precios y alcance acordados antes del trabajo.
- No se realizan cambios de hardware sin autorización.
- No se solicitan credenciales bancarias.
- Los datos del cliente no se copian fuera de su equipo salvo que sea necesario y expresamente autorizado.
- El trabajo y el plazo de responsabilidad se documentan por escrito.
- Documento tributario según corresponda.

## 2.3 What this is NOT

V1 is **not**:

- micro-soldering;
- motherboard component-level repair;
- advanced clean-room data recovery;
- liquid-damage board repair;
- screen/hinge/chassis repair requiring specialized parts unless explicitly accepted;
- phone repair;
- console repair;
- generic electronics repair;
- “we fix everything” positioning;
- 24/7 emergency IT;
- all-Santiago coverage.

If a case is outside the service boundary, say so quickly and, when possible, recommend the appropriate type of specialist.

# 3. Business objectives

## 3.1 First 30 days

Primary goals:

- Launch the site and acquisition assets.
- Generate **20 qualified leads**.
- Complete **10 paid jobs**.
- Obtain at least **6 genuine Google reviews** from real customers.
- Identify top 3 service categories by:
  - revenue per hour;
  - contribution margin;
  - lead-to-job conversion;
  - recurrence/referral potential.
- Measure actual acquisition source for at least 90% of leads.
- Establish a real baseline for travel time, average ticket and effective hourly income.

## 3.2 First 90 days

Targets to validate, not promises:

- 30–50 completed paid jobs.
- 15–25 genuine reviews.
- Average completed-job ticket ≥ CLP 35,000.
- Paid-job gross revenue per working hour ≥ CLP 25,000 before tax/transport.
- Travel time ideally ≤ 30 minutes each way.
- Organic/referral acquisition ≥ 60% of completed jobs.
- Repeat/referral jobs ≥ 20% by month three.
- Identify whether a small-business support offer deserves a separate product line.

## 3.3 Kill / change criteria

The model needs revision if, after enough observations:

- average effective revenue is below CLP 15,000/hour;
- average round-trip travel exceeds 75 minutes;
- more than 30% of leads request services outside scope;
- no acquisition channel reaches 20% lead-to-paid conversion;
- cost per acquired customer exceeds 25% of first-job gross margin;
- warranty/rework consumes more than 8% of service hours;
- support work materially damages higher-value professional opportunities without generating sufficient cash.

# 4. Target customers

Adults in Macul, Ñuñoa and selected Providencia sectors who:

- depend on a notebook/PC for work, study or personal administration;
- value convenience and trust more than the lowest possible price;
- prefer a technician to visit rather than transport equipment;
- need a clear explanation rather than technical jargon.

Common jobs:

- slow computer;
- overheating;
- boot errors;
- storage full;
- SSD/RAM upgrade;
- Windows problems;
- driver/peripheral problems;
- malware/security hygiene;
- backup/migration;
- new-PC setup;

Households needing help with:

- printers;
- routers;
- Wi-Fi coverage;
- backups;
- device setup;
- account/app configuration;
- migration from an old PC to a new PC;
- basic digital-safety configuration.

Examples:

- clinics/consultations;
- accountants;
- lawyers;

# 6. Service catalog — V1

Keep the visible catalog short enough for customers to understand.

## 6.1 A. Visita técnica + diagnóstico

**Provisional price**
- Macul / Ñuñoa: CLP 25,000
- Providencia covered sectors: CLP 30,000

Includes:

- on-site visit;
- basic diagnostic inspection;
- explanation of findings;
- up to a defined amount of simple troubleshooting that does not require disassembly, destructive changes or extended work;
- recommendation and written next step when further work is required.

Important: define internally whether the included troubleshooting window is 30 or 45 minutes. Recommended: **up to 45 minutes total on site** for the base visit.

Anything beyond that requires explicit approval.

## 6.2 B. Mantención y temperatura

Examples:

- dust removal;
- fan/heatsink cleaning;
- thermal-paste replacement where appropriate;
- temperature check before/after;
- fan/noise check;
- basic health report.

Provisional:
- desktop from CLP 30,000–35,000;
- notebook from CLP 35,000–45,000.

Final price depends on device complexity.

## 6.3 C. SSD / RAM upgrade

Includes:

- compatibility check;
- installation;
- BIOS/OS verification;
- basic post-install test.

Labor from CLP 25,000.

Data cloning/migration is a separate or bundled service.

For V1, prefer:
- customer buys recommended component through a link; or
- component procurement is explicitly agreed and prepaid.

Avoid holding inventory.

## 6.4 D. Windows / software recovery

Possible work:

- OS diagnosis;
- clean Windows installation;
- drivers;
- updates;
- basic configuration;
- restore from customer backup;
- startup optimization.

Provisional from CLP 35,000–45,000.

Windows licensing is never bypassed or pirated. Customer provides valid license entitlement where required.

## 6.5 E. Backup and data migration

Examples:

- old PC → new PC;
- HDD → SSD;
- local backup setup;
- external drive organization;
- OneDrive/Google Drive setup where requested.

Provisional from CLP 25,000–40,000 depending on volume and complexity.

Explicitly distinguish this from advanced data recovery.

## 6.6 F. Wi-Fi / home network

Examples:

- router setup;
- connectivity diagnosis;
- Wi-Fi coverage issues;
- extender/mesh configuration;
- DNS/device connectivity troubleshooting;
- printer/network device connection.

Provisional from CLP 30,000 for first intervention.

## 6.7 G. Remote support

Only for problems that do not require physical access.

Provisional:
- from CLP 15,000–20,000 for up to 45 minutes.

Use one-time session mechanisms where possible. Do not leave persistent unattended remote-access software installed by default.

# 7. Pricing model

## 7.1 Principles

Pricing must optimize for:

- clarity;
- low negotiation burden;
- reasonable margin;
- protection against “one small thing” turning into a two-hour visit;
- travel cost;
- differentiation from ultra-low-cost classifieds.

Never promise a fixed repair price before understanding the failure.

Use:

- exact base visit price;
- “desde” pricing for variable-complexity services;
- written quote for larger work.

## 7.2 Pricing experiments

Do not lock prices permanently before data.

Track:

- quote value;
- accepted/rejected;
- reason for rejection when volunteered;
- total minutes;
- travel minutes;
- consumables;
- rework;
- actual revenue/hour.

Review after:
- first 10 jobs;
- first 25 jobs;
- then monthly.

## 7.3 Parts

V1 preferred policy:

1. Diagnose.
2. Recommend exact specification/model.
3. Customer buys directly when practical.
4. Tooltician installs and tests.

If Tooltician procures:
- obtain approval first;
- require prepayment for the component;
- retain purchase evidence;
- state whether part is new/refurbished;
- itemize part and labor as required by applicable consumer rules.

# 8. Trust architecture

This is central to the product.

## 8.1 Real identity

Visible section:

**“Quién te atiende”**

Include:

- real professional photo;
- Carlos’s first and last name;
- concise professional description;
- links to Tooltician main site;
- LinkedIn;
- GitHub;
- selected public projects/proof.

Do not overshare private personal data.

## 8.2 Trust principles displayed on site

Suggested trust strip:

- Diagnóstico antes de intervenir
- Presupuesto antes de trabajos adicionales
- Atención personal
- Privacidad y datos respetados
- Trabajo documentado

## 8.3 Repair authorization

No destructive or material action without explicit customer authorization.

Require authorization for:

- formatting;
- OS reinstall;
- data deletion;
- account resets;
- partition changes;
- firmware/BIOS updates with meaningful risk;
- opening a device when not already agreed;
- part replacement;
- copying user data;
- sending a device off-site;
- purchasing parts.

## 8.4 Customer data

Default policy:

- do not ask for banking credentials;
- client enters passwords when practical;
- do not photograph or copy private files unless necessary and authorized;
- do not retain passwords;
- do not retain persistent remote access;
- do not upload customer files to third-party AI systems;
- no customer content in debugging screenshots unless necessary and authorized;
- if temporary data transfer is required, document location and deletion process.

## 8.5 Warranty / legal documentation

Before public launch, verify current Chilean consumer-law wording.

Operationally:

- every completed service receives a written record;
- state the period during which Tooltician assumes responsibility for the service/repair;
- distinguish labor responsibility from manufacturer/vendor warranty on parts;
- preserve evidence of parts used;
- identify whether a replacement component is new or refurbished;
- provide the applicable tax document.

Do not use “sin garantía” language to try to contract out of statutory consumer rights.

Classify:

- **A — likely on-site resolution**
- **B — diagnosis then quote**

- base price;

- parking/access notes;
- scope;
- accepted payment method.

## 9.7 On-site

Sequence:

1. confirm symptoms;
2. inspect;
3. record baseline;
4. diagnose;
5. explain;
6. get authorization;
7. perform work;
8. test;
9. show result;
10. issue service summary and payment document.

## 9.8 After-service

Send:

- short written summary;
- relevant recommendations;
- warranty/responsibility information;
- review request;
- optional referral request.

Never condition any discount, gift or benefit on a Google review.

# 13. Technical architecture

## 13.1 Principle

**Static-first. Minimal runtime. Minimal data collection.**

This product does not initially need:
- login;
- customer account;
- database;
- payment gateway;
- appointment engine;
- CRM backend.

## 13.2 Repo strategy

Work must inspect the existing Tooltician repository and deployment before choosing implementation.

Preferred decision order:

### Option A — same repository, separate app/deployment
Use if the existing repository supports a clean `apps/support` or equivalent structure.

Advantages:
- shared design tokens;
- shared components;
- one codebase;
- clean deployment separation.

### Option B — same site build with subdomain-specific entrypoint
Use only if the existing framework/deployment supports this cleanly without brittle host-routing hacks.

### Option C — separate repository
Use only if the current repo architecture makes the support vertical materially harder to maintain.

Default preference: **keep it near the existing Tooltician codebase without coupling deployment unnecessarily.**

## 13.3 Subdomain

Target:

`https://soporte.tooltician.com`

Requirements:
- valid TLS;
- forced HTTPS;
- no mixed content;
- canonical host;
- correct DNS;
- no duplicate site under deployment-provider URL if avoidable.

## 13.4 Rendering

Prefer static generation/SSR-free pages unless current stack provides a compelling reason otherwise.

No heavy framework additions solely for this vertical.

## 13.5 Contact mechanism

Primary:
- WhatsApp deep link.

Secondary:
- phone call only if desired;
- email only if useful.

Recommended V1:
- small client-side triage form;
- generate prefilled WhatsApp message;
- no server-side storage of the submitted problem.

## 13.6 Lead reference

Optional:
Generate a non-identifying short lead reference client-side and include it in the WhatsApp message, e.g.

`Ref: TS-8K4F`

This can later reconcile acquisition events with actual WhatsApp conversations.

Do not include sensitive customer information in URLs or analytics parameters.

# 22. Safety & triage rules

## 22.1 Stop-use red flags

- exposed mains damage;

- severe electrical short symptoms;

Before destructive work:

- offer backup/migration;

# 31. Decision rules

Examples:

### Raise price
If:
- booking conversion > 60%;
- capacity begins filling;
- effective hourly income exceeds target;
- almost no price objections.

### Reduce service area
If:
- travel time > 30–40% of total paid-work time for an area.

### Stop a channel
If:
- at least 10 qualified leads produce poor economics;
- CAC > 25% of first-job gross margin with no evidence of higher LTV.

### Productize a service
If:
- same problem repeats ≥ 5 times;
- scope is predictable;
- time variance is low;
- margin is attractive.

### Add recurring small-business support
If:
- ≥ 3 business customers independently ask for ongoing help.

# 36. Success definition

The project succeeds when it is no longer “a nice support website.”

It succeeds when:

1. a nearby customer can discover Tooltician Soporte;
2. understand the offer and price quickly;
3. verify who is behind it;
4. contact Carlos with low friction;
5. receive a predictable, trustworthy process;
6. complete a profitable job;
7. leave a genuine review or recommend the service;
8. and the resulting data tells us which service/channel should receive the next hour and peso.

That creates a local acquisition flywheel:

**visibility → trust → contact → good service → review/referral → stronger visibility → lower CAC → better customers.**

The website is the conversion and trust layer.  
The operating process is the product.  
The reviews and referrals become the moat.
